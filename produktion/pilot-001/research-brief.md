**Investigation closed:** yes — final report published. Mandatory condition met.

**Core question of the video:** A power system does not fail because one thing
breaks. It fails because the people responsible do not know it is breaking.
What did the operators in Akron actually see on their screens between 2 p.m.
and 4 p.m. — and what did they not see?

**Search potential:** high. The event is a fixed reference point ("2003
blackout", "Northeast blackout"), searched continuously, and the technical
story is far less covered than the anecdotal one.

## The decision point

**14:14 — the alarms stop, and nobody is told.**

The obvious candidate for the decision point is a tree contact, or the
Sammis–Star trip that begins the cascade. Both are wrong for this format,
because by then the outcome was largely determined.

The moment the situation became unrecoverable is the moment the control room
lost the ability to see it. The alarm processor failed in the worst possible
way: silently. From that point the operators were not making bad decisions —
they were making reasonable decisions about a grid that no longer existed on
their screens. Every later event is a consequence of that blindness.

This also matches the report's own framing: two of the four grouped causes are
about knowing, not about equipment — **inadequate system understanding** and
**inadequate situational awareness**. **[S]**

## Cause as stated in the report

The Task Force grouped the causes into four: **[S]** `[VERIFY p. __]`

1. Inadequate system understanding
2. Inadequate situational awareness
3. Inadequate tree trimming
4. Inadequate reliability coordinator diagnostic support

The report issued **46 recommendations**. **[S]** `[VERIFY p. __]`

> **Note for the script:** the ordering matters and must not be rearranged for
> narrative effect. Tree trimming is third of four, not first — the popular
> version of this story ("a tree caused the blackout") inverts the report's
> own emphasis. That inversion is the story.

## What was claimed at the time that the report does not support

- **The Blaster worm.** The MSBlaster worm was spreading in August 2003, and a
  connection to the blackout was widely speculated. The Task Force examined
  the question specifically and did not find that it caused the outage. **[K]**
  `[VERIFY p. __ — this needs the exact wording; "did not cause" and "found no
  evidence that" are different claims and the difference matters]`
- **A lightning strike.** Circulated in early reporting; not the cause per the
  report. **[K]** `[VERIFY p. __]`
- **"It started in Canada."** Early attributions pointed across the border;
  the sequence begins in northern Ohio. **[K]** `[VERIFY p. __]`

## Facts to visualise

The channel draws its own graphics; this section describes *what must be
shown*, not how it should look.

1. **The 345 kV corridor in northern Ohio** — schematic, not geographic:
   Eastlake, Chamberlin–Harding, Hanna–Juniper, Star–South Canton,
   Sammis–Star. Lines drop out one at a time as the timeline advances.
2. **Load transfer** — as each 345 kV line trips, its load moves to the
   remaining lines and to the 138 kV network. This is the mechanism of the
   whole event and needs to be *seen*, not described.
3. **The two views, side by side** — the actual state of the grid versus what
   the control room display showed after 14:14. The gap between the two panels
   is the emotional core of the video.
4. **Cascade propagation** — the final minutes, as an outline map with a
   time counter.
5. **Sag geometry** — why a heavily loaded line heats, sags, and reaches a
   tree that it cleared an hour earlier.

## Risks

- **Blame.** Individual operators are identifiable in this story. The report
  investigates system failure, not personal fault, and the script must follow
  it. No names.
- **The tree simplification.** "A tree caused it" is memorable and wrong. The
  script must resist it even though it is the easier line.
- **Unverified page references** — see the provenance warning at the top.
